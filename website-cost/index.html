Why the prices are all over the map

Two websites can look similar and cost ten times apart. The difference is almost never the colors. It is what happens under the hood: how many pages are written from scratch, whether the site is built for search, whether it sells products, books appointments or answers questions at midnight.

Here is the practical way to think about it: you are not buying pages, you are buying outcomes. A $3,000 site that never produces a lead is expensive. A site that books two extra jobs a month can be the cheapest thing you ever bought.

The hidden costs nobody puts on the invoice

Your time. A DIY site is cheap in dollars and expensive in evenings. If you would rather spend that time with customers, a done-for-you build often pays for itself.

Lost leads. A slow or confusing site quietly sends visitors to competitors. You never see those losses on a statement, but they are real. Our speed test shows how your current site does on a phone.

Rebuilds. Cheap sites often get rebuilt within two years. Paying twice is the most expensive way to buy a website.

Invisible SEO debt. A site built with no search structure has to be fixed later before it can rank. Want to know where yours stands? Start with a free SEO audit.

How much does a website cost per month?

Why do some web designers charge so much more?

Price usually reflects custom writing, strategy, search structure, features like ecommerce or booking, and how much of the site is built from scratch. Ask every designer what outcome the site is built to produce.

Is a cheap website worth it?

It can be, if it is fast, clear and built to produce calls. Many cheap sites cost more in lost leads and rebuilds than they save upfront.

What should a website quote include?

Pages and content, platform, search structure, speed targets, forms and tracking, hosting, who owns what, and what happens after launch.
