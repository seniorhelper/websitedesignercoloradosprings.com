Compliance language

Talk about CMMC carefully

The Department of Defense's CMMC rule for contracts (the DFARS clauses) took effect on November 10, 2025, with a phased rollout. According to law-firm summaries of the rule, the second phase, beginning November 10, 2026, adds third-party Level 2 certification as a condition of award for many contracts that involve Controlled Unclassified Information.

For your website, that means two things. First, describe your cybersecurity posture accurately; claiming a level you have not achieved can cost you far more than it gains. Second, keep your public site out of scope by design: no uploads of contract documents, no forms that ask for sensitive program details. Your compliance advisor decides your obligations; your website should simply never get in the way.

Background: GRF CPAs on the DFARS CMMC final rule; Mondaq summary of the final rule. This is general information, not legal or compliance advice.

Contractor website questions

What should a government contractor website include?

At minimum: a capability statement page with your UEI, CAGE code, NAICS codes and any set-aside status; core competencies; past performance you are allowed to share; a named point of contact; and a downloadable one-page capability statement. Those details should match your SAM.gov registration exactly.

Should my UEI and CAGE code be on my website?

Most contractors list them, because contracting officers and primes use them to look you up quickly. Make sure they match SAM.gov character for character.

Does my public website need to be CMMC compliant?

CMMC applies to systems that process, store or transmit Federal Contract Information or Controlled Unclassified Information. Your public marketing site should never handle that kind of information, so keep it off the site and its forms. Do not claim a CMMC level on your site until you have actually achieved it. Talk to your compliance advisor about your specific obligations.

Does a contractor website need to meet Section 508?

Section 508 governs the technology federal agencies build, buy and use. Your marketing site may not be covered directly, but building to WCAG 2.1 AA, the standard Section 508 references, shows buyers you understand accessibility and makes your site usable for everyone.

Can you build a careers page for cleared positions?

Yes. We build careers pages that state clearance requirements plainly, explain your hiring steps, and add job posting schema so roles can appear in search results. Sensitive details stay out of public listings.
